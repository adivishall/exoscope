## 2. Null and false-positive tests

- **Pure noise** (30 light curves, 800 ppm noise, no transit): 0/30 were reported as a significant signal (checklist verdict other than “no significant signal”).
- **Eclipsing-binary imitation** (30 light curves, alternating 8000/4000 ppm eclipses): 30/30 were flagged as “signs of a non-planetary origin” by the odd/even or secondary-eclipse checks.

## 4. Injection–recovery on real light curves

Simulated 3-hour transits were multiplied into **real** Kepler light curves after masking each star's own planet (Kepler-10 b, Kepler-8 b) — depths [50, 100, 200, 400, 800] ppm × periods [1.7, 4.3, 7.9] d × 4 phases per host. The same injections were made into a *white-noise twin* (same timestamps, same point-to-point scatter, Gaussian noise). Recovered = BLS best period within 1% of the injected period **and** SNR ≥ 7.1 (ExoScope's detection threshold). Data with injections are labelled “injected”, never “real observation”.

## Limitations of this evaluation

- Synthetic light curves use white noise; real stars have correlated (red) noise and instrumental systematics, so synthetic precision/recall are optimistic.
- Real-data labels only cover *known* planets. Agreement with an archive period shows the method recovers known signals; it does not measure the false-positive rate on stars without planets.
- The real-target sample is small and biased towards easy, deep hot Jupiters plus one small planet (Kepler-10 b).
- “Legacy v1” is a reconstruction of the original method described in the project brief, applied to the same preprocessed light curve.
